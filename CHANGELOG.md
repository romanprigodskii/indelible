# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- **Level 4, upkeep and sentinels are scheduled.** Mastery 4 needs a second cold pass a week after the first, and `plan.md` promised a sentinel about 4 weeks after a mistake retires, but nothing ever listed either, so level 4 and 5 were out of reach and owned topics went unretrieved until the mocks. `ind due --list` now has three more tiers after "oldest due": 5, the sentinel of each retired mistake 28 days after its last pass (or a week before the date), read from the archive too, until one is graded; 6, the level-4 recheck of a topic at 3, from 7 days after its first pass; 7, an upkeep recheck of a topic at 4 or 5, 21 days after its last pass, brought forward to 2 days before the date and never on or after it. "Needs repair" is tier 8 (`8_needs_repair` in `--json`). DUE counts them ("later rechecks ready now", "last check"). Rule R5 again in `method.md`, `session-open.md` §5, `plan.md` §6, `measure.md` §8, the builder's SERVE lines, the README's method and the contract say so.
- **A recheck that leaves a topic below mastery 3 comes round again.** After a topic's first 2-day recheck nothing served it cold again, so a topic that missed it stayed at 2 even after its mistake was fixed and passed four spaced returns, and topics built on it stayed gated. Now a topic whose last recheck left it below 3 gets a new 44–72 h window from its next warm exposure (the fix sheet, the logged feedback, drills), and `ind due --list` offers it in tier 1, marked "again"; lint L7, the "Start by" line, `plan check` and the late-recheck flags judge it by that window, as a first serve. `grade record` names each topic a recheck left below 3 and what opens its window, and `error repair` recomputes the levels before it moves a window, so a topic held at 2 only by that mistake is not treated as one. `measure.md` §8, `session-open.md` §5, `session-grade.md` and the contract say so.
- **A fix comes at least 24 hours before its recheck, not 12.** Method rule R6, `plan.md` §4 and §5 and close check C5 asked for a repair at least 12 h before the recheck it serves, but a repair is an exposure, and the 24-hour rule takes its topic off any recheck less than 24 h later. All of them now say 24 h: C5 flags a recheck within 24 h that holds a topic with an unfixed mistake, and says to move the recheck or serve it without the topic. The contract gives `repair`'s due date as the next day, with the 24-hour rule setting the earliest serve.
- **Practice sheets say the learner may ask for a hint.** The hint ladder and the worked-example fallback start only when the learner speaks up, but every drill sheet said "no AI" and nothing said they could ask, so a stuck novice sat in silence or wrote "I don't know" throughout. On theory, external, example, repair and drills sheets, the "I don't know" line now adds "Stuck on a question after a real try? Tell me its number: you get a small hint, never the answer", and drills say "no other AI". Mixed, review and measuring sheets are unchanged. The drills hand-over and the welcome card say the same, and the floundering timeout counts from the learner's first ask, which Claude can see. The sample workspace was rebuilt.
- **Only the session-open brief counts an open.** Every `ind brief <s>` used to count one for the "not taken after 2 opens" rule and write `data/sheets.jsonl`, so two status looks a few hours apart could bring "Sit them now, or drop that sheet?". `ind brief` now writes nothing; `ind brief <s> --open`, run in session-open step 1, counts. The flag reads "not taken after 2 sessions". Status really changes nothing, as the README says.
- **`topics.json` no longer stores `cold_passes`.** The list was filled by any sitting at 75% or more, including one-question, out-of-window and practice sittings that the level rules reject, and nothing read it. Cold passes are found in `attempts.jsonl` by the level rules, and `level_basis` names the one that counts. An older workspace's list is left as it is and ignored. The sample workspace was rebuilt.
- **The exam date question states the published format instead of asking for it.** The exam Q2 card asked about ten things, among them attempts and retake cost, which nothing records or uses, and a public exam's full format, which Claude can state. It now asks the date (or window), registration, start time and accommodations, then says "I'll plan to the published format: … [published]; say if yours differs"; with no public format, or one Claude isn't sure of, it asks for the format instead and never states one from memory. Q4 (materials) had only exam wording, so a language or code learner was asked about unseen official papers: it now has a language line (an app, a book, a course, a phrasebook) and a code or skill line (a book, course or tutorial to follow). `profiles.md` no longer mentions retake questions.
- **An on-demand learner's return is one short opener.** After two weeks away, persona D got the re-entry session, whose last step re-plans a week an on-demand learner doesn't have, and the weekly review's 3-line summary was never wired to the open, and counted what was due ("Due now: one 2-day recheck and 3 reviews"), which re-entry bans. On demand, re-entry now skips the week re-plan (the close names the next window as usual), and its opener carries the 3 lines, which count nothing due and ask nothing; the first comes from the brief's LAST SESSIONS alone (sessions and questions, no recheck rate), since the open may not run `ind stats`: the third is a statement (Say "change …" any time). The why line uses the learner's own words and is skipped when all there is is a one-word label.
- **"How am I doing" is a status question, and status has a shape.** The trigger sent a casual question on the train to the weekly review: three check-in questions, a `reviews/` file, compaction and proposals. Status had no answer shape, so replies varied and could show raw metric names, and nothing told Claude to keep the brief's private block to itself, so a status look before a recheck could name its topics. "How am I doing" now routes to status (`SKILL.md`, `README.md`, `review.md`); the review's triggers are "weekly review", "review my week" or a yes to the close offer. A new `review.md` §9 gives status at most 5 plain lines (this week's sessions, the latest 2-day recheck, what's solid, the next session, one to-do), never reads out anything below the line, translates stats labels, writes nothing, and ends with "Full weekly review? (yes/no)". Learners with sessions of 30 minutes or less get the weekly review as 3 lines with that offer, as the week-1 "3-line check-in" in `measure.md` §11 promised; the check-in questions come only on a yes. `close.md` §8, read at every close, now makes that offer (or gives the 3 lines) at the first close of a new week; nothing made it before, so the review ran only when asked for by name.
- **The learner is told how photos reach the session.** Paper plus phone photos is the main answer route for exams and courses, but nothing said how a photo gets from the phone into the terminal, and the P8 offer of a synced photos folder had no follow-through. The welcome card now has a photos line fitted to the learner's computer and phone (AirDrop or the inbox folder on a Mac; email the photos to yourself and save them in the inbox on Windows; attach them in Claude Code desktop; a paste works too, but keeps only a transcript), and the recheck, drills and diagnostic hand-overs point to it. When the learner says "sent", `session-grade.md` §2 lists image and PDF files newer than the sheet in the inbox, Downloads and any recorded photos folder, names them, and files them only after a yes. P8 now asks for a folder just for study photos and records its path in the root `CLAUDE.md`. `PRIVACY.md` says that those file names reach the conversation.
- **Marking names each question by what it asked, not its number alone.** The verdict list, the account menu and every feedback line said "Question 4", even for a sheet sat days earlier (a solo block, marking carried over, a photo sent later), so a learner who does several sheets a day had to dig out the old sheet or gave no account. `session-grade.md` §3 now names each question by its number, a gist of at most 12 words from the visible spec (never the key) and the learner's answer as filed: "4 (median of six waiting times; you wrote 11.2)". An official item, which has only a pointer in the spec, is named by its number and the learner's answer, never the official one. The account menu, feedback, the result card and diagnostic accounts use the same form; the Right line stays numbers only. A vocabulary probe quotes the sentence the word came from instead of "in question 6", and "Which questions?" before a recheck is marked adds "Topics in your own words are fine."
- **After a slip, Claude names the check instead of asking the learner for it.** The quantitative taxonomy's careless treatment still said "ask which check would have caught it", a last quiz after the pointer that `session-grade.md` §7, where Claude names the check form, had already dropped. It now reads: point to the line and the learner's value ("last line: your 0.35"), then name the check that would have caught it. `session-grade.md` §6 adds the value to the pointer when the miss is a number: the learner's value, never the right one, so the corrected line stays theirs.
- **The first-teach warning covers sittings and prompt suggestions.** It asked the learner not to expand Claude's tool calls or thinking only while sheets were built, but a sheet's answers are also in the conversation while the learner works it and at marking, and the app's prompt suggestions are written from the conversation too. The one-time line in `teach.md` §8 now covers working a sheet and asks the learner to turn prompt suggestions off in `/config`.
- **A mock is sat without check lines, as the exam is.** Every mock, and every official question on a diagnostic or checkpoint, needed a written check beside each answer (lint L2 failed one without), while `measure.md` asks for exam conditions and the exam has no check column: at about 90 seconds a question, copying the supporting words took a large share of the clock, so a mock measured a slower task than the exam, and a checkpoint's pre-registered threshold, whose `if_below` is binding, was applied to it. L2 now passes a `mock` with no check lines, and an official question (`origin: official:`) on a diagnostic or checkpoint; a question Claude wrote there keeps its check. The rules box of a mock, or of a sheet of official questions only, says "Check your answers as you would in the exam: there is no check line on this sheet.", and a sheet where only some questions have one says "where a Check line is printed". Such questions are graded `check: n/a`, which check coverage leaves out. `builder.md` rule 4, `sheets.md` §1–§3 and §7, `profiles.md`, `measure.md` §3, §5, §6 and §12, `session-grade.md` §7, method rule R41 and §6, the README and the contract say so.
- **Feedback never tallies the learner's repeats.** Law 10 banned praise, sarcasm and "simply", but not counts of the learner's repeats ("that's the fourth time", "every session this week"), a person-level judgement that names no error; in the method's development such tallies were frequent, several of the "habits" counted turned out to come from the sheets' own design, and they fed the learner's frustration. Law 10 (in `SKILL.md` and the contract) now says never to tally the learner's past misses, and the `session-grade.md` §6 Don't table has the row; a miss on a mistake's own re-serve may still be named as a fact about its fix, with no count. `taxonomies.md` §7 has repeats checked first for a shared item design (the last sub-item, a printed formula, a multi-part chat question, an undefined word, a late block), which is logged as Claude's defect with a structural fix; otherwise one structural change is proposed at the weekly review, never a reminder. `review.md` §6 and method's Law 10 row point to it.
- **A label says what form counts, and marking holds to the exam's form.** The key accepted every defensible form, but nothing told the learner so, and nothing covered the form an exam requires: a learner who believed an answer isn't finished until its brackets are opened spent the clock on algebra, while an answer in the learner's own name for a standard rule was marked right though the exam would reject it. `builder.md` rule 4 now has each label say "any equivalent form" or name the one form wanted, and on exam and course subjects the exam's required form in its own wording (`sheets.md` §5 has the Don't row). `session-grade.md` §9 says "defensible" means what the exam's marking accepts: a right answer in a form the exam won't take is `half`, answer form (`F`), a slip, with the exam's form said in one line, and an `item_wording` defect when the label never named it; the quantitative taxonomy's F row has an own-name example. No standing rules-box line was added: "form" means a verb form on a language sheet.
- **The Least-sure line asks about the idea, not a slip.** Its label, "Least sure of (item numbers)", said nothing about which doubt it meant, so a careful learner named the items where an arithmetic slip was possible, which the method itself calls the wrong signal, and a named right answer then cost its level. The label is now "Least sure I chose the right idea (item numbers):", one wording for every subject, and every reference, the README and `SKILL.md` use it. The rules box's time line adds "Then fill in the Least-sure line: item numbers, or “none”.", so a line left blank no longer reads as "none"; the box stays at 8 lines. The sample workspace was rebuilt.
- **Naming a right answer on the Least-sure line costs a delay, not the level.** Named questions never counted toward mastery, even when right, and `--shaky` opened one mistake per question. So an item named on the only in-window 2-day recheck held its topic at 2 even after every +3-day re-serve came back right, with a burst of re-serves for one doubt, and leaving the line blank became the learner's best move. `grade record --shaky` now opens one shaky mistake per named item and topic, holding all its right named answers. When that mistake's first counted re-serve comes back right, those answers count toward the level at their own sitting, so the recheck can still make 3; a miss there leaves them out, and a right answer is never counted as wrong. `grade record` then names the recheck that now counts, sets it as the topic's last recheck and closes its booking. `method.md` §4, `measure.md` §8, `sheets.md` §4 and the contract say so.
- **A locked override of R12 order lets the rule come first.** R12 order (a concrete case before the definition) is a default a learner may override at review, but lint L11 refused every theory or repair sheet with the rule before its worked case, so the override could never take effect. L11 now accepts that order when the subject's `overrides` holds a locked override of `R12 order` (or of `R12`, saved before it was split); a sheet with no worked case still fails. `method.md` §6, `session-teach.md` §2, `builder.md` rule 8, `sheets.md` §7 and the contract say so.
- **A floor the diagnostic called "Already yours" is enough to build on.** The floor check asked for "mastery 3 or above", but a diagnostic can give at most 3p, which the CLI ranks below 3, so a learner told "Already yours: z-scores" was then told z-scores "isn't solid yet", and "the new topic next time" was wrong for any floor that needs a 2-day recheck first. The floor check in `session-teach.md` §2 now asks for level 3p or above; a floor below it is served first, and the new topic waits until that floor's 2-day recheck has passed. `measure.md` §10 calls a floor owned at the same line, `close.md` §9 runs the floor check before building the next theory ahead (and builds the floor topic's sheets instead), and the weekly discrimination sheet for a confusable pair counts 3p too (`session-teach.md` §5, `plan.md`).
- **The hint ladder says where a pointer comes from.** Rungs 3 and 4 ("look at the verb in the second sentence", "one step further") need the question, but nothing said how Claude gets it during a sitting: the build step said not to review a sheet's content, and working the question out would put its answer in Claude's thinking before anything is filed, which Law 1 forbids. `session-teach.md` §3 now says: before anything is filed, hint only from what the learner has written or quoted; on drills, file that question's work alone (`scan ingest --asks 5a`, as a gate photo is filed), so `key open` prints only its entry and the pointer comes from its solution; on theory, external, example and repair sheets, point to the sheet's own worked case, read from the visible sheet (never `.indelible/`). Pencil marking names the step from the visible theory sheet too. `sheets.md` §6 limits "don't review the sheet's content" to build time, and `sheets.md` §10, `session-grade.md` §2, the `--asks` help and the contract name the hint filing.
- **A sentence question follows the numbers it is about.** Builder rule 3 put a sentence or verbal item first on every drills sheet, "while the learner is fresh", and lint W2 warned otherwise; in the method's development, sentence questions set apart from their numbers were missed while the computed questions beside them were right, and the same content asked as numbers first, then one sentence inside the item, landed. On a drills sheet with both computed and sentence questions, each sentence question is now the last question of the item whose numbers it uses, and names them ("Using your answers to 3a–3b, …"). W2 warns on a sentence item with no computed item before it on its topic, and passes a sheet of sentence or reading items only. New method rule R51 ([one-learner]) and a `method.md` §6 note give the reason; `builder.md`, `session-teach.md` §4, `sheets.md` §1 and §7 and the contract say so.
- **Worked cases carry a principle prompt.** R12 cited the fading research, but the skill took only its fading half: its one self-explanation prompt was an open "why does this step follow?" on drill item 1, and theory, repair and example worked cases had none, though in that research prompts to name the principle behind each worked step are what carried the method to unfamiliar questions (Atkinson, Renkl & Merrill, 2003). Every worked case (theory, repair, example, and drills item 1 at mastery 0–1) now gets one or two keyed choice questions on a named step, "Step 2 of the worked case is done because: (a) … (b) … (c) …", answered by letter, the options being the rule, the contrast's wrong idea and the warning's wrong turn, all on the sheet; on a verbal subject a focused "why not <the tempting option>" may stand in on drills. They are marked like any pencil, count in `est_min`, and never go on measuring sheets. R12 reads "…with a principle prompt on each worked case" and cites both studies; `builder.md` rules 7 and 8, `session-teach.md` §1, §2 and its "Why this shape" line, and `method.md` §6 say so. The sample theory sheet has one.
- **On a new topic the failure gate stops every time.** The gate after item 3 fired only on 2 failed checks, "I don't know"s or blanks, but a wrong idea passes its own check (the mean put back into the mean formula holds), so a novice could practise it through a whole block, often two, before any feedback; and in sessions of 30 minutes or less, marking of new drills could carry over to the open of the day that topic's recheck was due, just before it or not at all. A drills block with a topic below mastery 2 now carries `gate: "always"` (lint L6 requires it; no level on file counts as 0), and its sheet prints "Stop here and send a photo of items 1–3. Go on once I've marked them.": Claude files those items, opens only their key, gives the verdicts, the account and the standard for each miss, and repairs a wrong idea before item 4. Such drills are marked in the session they are sat, and `sheet issue` refuses them on a solo block, which has nobody to mark them, so a new topic is taught at a session with Claude. In short sessions, drill questions are cut rather than their marking; marking that still carries over happens at the open, before the recheck is built, and logs a `review` exposure so the topic comes off that day's recheck. R36 and `method.md` §6 cite Shute (2008); `builder.md` rule 7, `session-teach.md` §4 and §6, `session-open.md` §4, `close.md` §6 and §8, `plan.md`, `sheets.md` §1, §2 and §7, the schema and the contract say so. The sample drills stop after item 4 for marking. The sample workspace was rebuilt.
- **A recheck is warned when it asks for an operation never shown, and every pencil names its operation.** Lint L12 tied a drills sheet's operations to its theory, but a 2-day recheck could still ask for an operation no sheet of the topic had worked or practised, and a theory pencil with no `op` left the drills nothing to match. New warning W7 names a `cold:` item whose `op` no earlier theory, external, example, repair or drills sheet of its topic used (a topic with no teaching sheet is skipped, and a recheck is never blocked); builder rule 1 keeps recheck items to operations the topic's sheets used. L12 now also fails a pencil question with no `op` on a theory, external, example or repair sheet. No exemption was added for a fully worked item: a drills block holds one operation, so it could never change the result. `builder.md` rules 1 and 8, `sheets.md` §7 and the contract say so.
- **Drills sat without Claude follow the same marking rule.** `session-open.md` step 2 and `session-teach.md` §6 marked a solo block's photos after the day's recheck, while marking of new drills carried over to the next open now comes before it: drills on a topic whose first 2-day recheck is still ahead are now marked in the open, before the recheck is built, in both places, so the recheck is never sat on drills nobody has marked.
- **A learner who insists on a recheck too soon gets practice, and the recheck stays booked.** `session-open.md` said the 24-hour rule and the recheck window could be overridden with the result labelled, but the only instrument it named, a `cold` sheet, can't pass lint L7, and every workaround (another type, another origin) is banned, while `plan.md` described a different route. Now one route: the booked recheck stays as it is, and the questions the learner asked for come as a `mixed` practice sheet whose items are all `origin: new` (never a `cold:` or `error:` item), shown as "[practice] not counted (seen too recently)" and logged with `session override`; grading logs a drill exposure, then `plan check` says whether the recheck must move. `SKILL.md`'s ban, `session-open.md` §3 step 6 and §5, `plan.md` §4, the L7 row in `sheets.md` and `builder.md` rule 12 say the same; the gap between measurements keeps its own route (`measure.md` §4).
- **A quick session keeps its recheck, and an on-demand session can teach.** A quick session was "an unplanned drop-in, or one shorter than the learner's usual length" serving "nothing new and no measurement", which covered every on-demand session (they have no planned blocks), forbade the first teach `measure.md` §11 gives an on-demand learner, made a 60-minute drop-in by a 120-minute learner a session with nothing new, and could be read as dropping the 2-day recheck, which is a measurement. `session-open.md` §4 now defines it for scheduled mode only: opened with no block and under half of `session.length_min`, it serves the 2-day recheck if its window is open, then the other due items in tier order; a new topic only as an override (`session override`), sized to the minutes left; no diagnostic, mock or checkpoint. A longer drop-in is an ordinary session, and on-demand sessions are never quick. `sheets.md` §6 says which sessions issue sheets with no `--block`.
- **Missed sessions are asked about in one message.** The open asked about each missed block in its own message, then "what got in the way" after each skip, then the alarm's three choices, so two missed commutes could cost a 20-minute train session five exchanges, on the very "missed a couple of days, felt behind" that had made persona B quit before; it never used what the learner had already said, and it listed the misses before re-entry's promise not to. `session-open.md` step 2 now asks about all of them at once, by day ("Monday's and Tuesday's sessions have no record…"), records what the learner already said without asking, asks "what got in the way" at most once per open and only after a skip with no reason, and at re-entry neither lists nor counts them (one question, only when a block was the learner's own). The three choices come in their own message and, for the subject being opened, only once two skips in a row are confirmed. `plan.md` §2 and §7, `session-teach.md` §6 and question P6 say the same, and `plan.md` no longer calls persona B's commute sessions soft blocks, which are never asked about.
- **A recheck whose window has passed no longer blocks the plan.** `plan check` failed a recheck that was never placed and whose window had closed, and one placed after its window, though nothing done while planning can fix either: the late-recheck rule runs at the next session. `plan.md` and `calendar.md` allow no preview and no calendar write until the check passes, so re-planning was blocked for days, and the pull was toward an early `plan cancel`, which also clears the brief's LATE RECHECK flag. Both are now a WARN, `late_recheck`, with the same fix, so the plan passes and the WARN becomes a preview line. A recheck placed outside a window that is still open still fails. The contract and `plan.md` §4 say so.
- **Moving the recheck asks for no prediction.** A "skip the recheck" override moves the recheck, but `session-open.md` still asked for a one-line prediction about specific questions of a sheet not yet built, which the learner had never seen, so any answer was a total in disguise. It now gets no prediction and no `session override`: nothing is sat, and the move is the record. Law 9, the contract's copy and method rule R34 say so.
- **`session extend` checks the next fixed start too.** An extension is capped by min(`session.extension_max_min`, a quarter of the planned minutes, the next fixed start less 15 minutes less the planned end), but the command enforced only the first two, and the third rested on Claude reading the day's blocks, blocked time and bedtime by hand, so an extension could run into the next subject's block or the half hour before bed. It now finds the next fixed start itself (another open block of any subject, a `time.blocked` span, or 30 minutes before bedtime, counting only what starts once the session is under way) and refuses an extension past it, naming the start and the cap; before another block it says to close or to move that block with the learner's yes. `close.md` §2 and the contract say so.
- **Re-entry asks for the learner's reason once when the notes have none.** The re-entry session opens with the learner's own "why", from the learner notes the brief prints, and skipped the line when it was missing there, so a learner coming back after a week away, the moment the reason matters most, heard nothing of it. It now asks once ("in a line, why does this matter to you?"), lets the answer stand in for the line, and records it under "Learner notes" in the subject `CLAUDE.md` once the recheck is handed over, so the next brief carries it. It is never looked up in `subject.json` at the open (Law 6); "skip", or a one-word label, leaves the line out.
- **A learner who answers from a phone has a route the rules allow.** Persona B reads sheets and types answers on a phone, but `profiles.md` and `sheets.md` said typed answers go "in a file, never chat", the recheck hand-over asked for paper and a photo, marking suggested a typed file a phone can't make, and the HTML sheet itself said "Answer on paper". With `format.answer_form` "typed", the rules box in every format now asks for the answers typed, numbered as on the sheet with their checks, and sent in one message (or a text file) when the learner stops, with the start and stop times and the Least-sure line; a failure gate asks for "your answers to" its three items, not a photo. The message is filed verbatim with `ind scan ingest <s> <id> --typed -` (evidence kind `typed`, not a photo transcript). The recheck hand-over, readback row 9, `sheets.md` §8 and §10, `session-grade.md` §2, `session-teach.md` and `profiles.md` say so. The sheet is still handed over as an HTML or Markdown file; a theory sheet pasted into chat because the file won't open sends its drills to a new conversation or a later session (Law 2).
- **The language placement fits a 20-minute session.** It was one `probe` of 40 words in sentences, 5 can-do prompts and a listening item, billed as 10 minutes, but a probe holds at most 10 questions, an honest estimate at the default paces came to about 66 minutes, and `sheet issue` refused it in persona B's 20-minute block. It is now two probes, in sessions 1 and 2, pooled as one measurement (the CLI already pools probes sat within 72 h): at most 8 high-frequency words in sentences, answered with a one-word gloss, then 3 can-do prompts answered in one typed line each, plus a listening item when there is audio. Both go in session 1 when its work minutes hold them. `measure.md` §1 and §11 (B's first teach moves to day 3, its recheck to day 6), `profiles.md` and the persona B fixture say so.
- **The daily 15-minute slot for long sessions is a logged catch-up, not a review.** Learners on 120-minute sessions were offered "a daily 15-minute review" with no content and no timing, and as worded it could send persona C, whose risk is cramming, to reread her notes each night, an unlogged exposure that voids the next morning's recheck. It is now a 15-minute catch-up, offered in the preview as an if-then plan in the learner's words: a logged quick session (`ind session open <s> --planned 15`, no block) that serves due rechecks and mistakes only, never new material and never rereading notes. Taken up, it goes under Learner notes. `teach.md` Q6, `session-open.md` §4, a `catch-up` row in `method.md` (opt, [design]) and the persona C fixture say so.
- **The materials question has an interview wording.** Q4 gave interviews, vivas and oral exams the exam wording, so a learner preparing for a panel was asked about unseen official past papers and question banks. It now asks what they are preparing from: the job advert or brief, their own work (a thesis, a portfolio, a project) and any list of likely questions, plus a book or course to follow; model answers are kept sealed until marking.

### Added

- **Every sheet carries a code to match its photo.** Titles are neutral by design, so every subject's recheck is called "2-day recheck", and nothing on a sheet or a notebook page tied a photo to its sheet. ## [0.1.5] - 2026-09-28

The learner is never sent to find their own mistake.

### Changed

- **Marking points to the error, and the learner makes the fix.** The account asked for each miss is now how the learner got their answer (`1) I did it this way: ___  2) a word stopped me  3) I guessed  4) I can see my slip: ___`), not "what happened?". On a topic met that day, "what happened?" asked them to find an error they couldn't see. Claude then names the question and the step, and asks once for the corrected line. A slip whose fix doesn't come is treated as a wrong idea, and a wrong idea gets its fix sheet. Law 11 and a new ban in `SKILL.md` say so, and "one of these is wrong: find it" is gone for good. Method rule R42 and `method.md` §5 give the reasons (Große & Renkl, 2007; Baars et al., 2014).
- **Careless needs more than a named rule.** Naming the right method counts as a slip account only when the learner writes the corrected line unaided once the step is pointed to. The same operation must also be done right, unaided, elsewhere (never the worked items 1–2 of a first drill). A guess not forced by time becomes the wrong idea "no method yet for …", and its fix sheet gives a second worked case in place of the contrast.
- **A failed check is a flag, not a hunt.** The rules box on sheets with check lines now says: "If a check fails and you can't see why within a minute, keep your answer, put its number on the Least-sure line and go on: I'll show you where at marking."
- **Checks on a new topic are ones the learner can run.** A theory or fix sheet's worked case now ends with its check, worked as a step. So does item 1 of a first drill block, which covers textbook pages. The drills' check hints name that check. "Another way" and "the weakest step" wait until a topic reaches mastery 3, because a new learner has one method and no sense yet of where they are weak. Rechecks give only the form of a check, never a topic's own method, so the check doesn't label the question.
- **"Which line is the first wrong one?" and "find the error" items** appear only on topics the learner owns, or where finding errors is the exam's own question form. Below that, the wrong working sits beside the right one. A mock's miss-review asks why the learner chose their answer, and asks for the answer now only after Claude has pointed to the line or step.
- The sample workspace was rebuilt: the new rules-box line, and the `.ics` PRODID now carries the current version.

### Added

- **Sheet checker rules.** L10 (fails): a check hint that asks for a search ("find your mistake", "check your work for mistakes"), a re-solve ("redo", "double-check") or a confidence rating, or says only "check your answer". Subject words such as "the standard error", "error bars" or "the error message" pass. W3 (warns): on a topic below mastery 3, a check line with no hint, or one that needs a second method or the weakest step. W4 (warns): a theory or repair sheet whose worked case has no "Check:" step.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-09-25

The first release. Built for Claude Code (terminal or desktop).

### Added

- **The skill.** `SKILL.md` with 14 laws, the absolute bans, a router, a command table (`teach`, `session`, `close`, `status`, `diagnose` / `mock`, `plan` / `reschedule`, `review`, `sync` and `migrate`) and a manual mode for machines without Python. Each command has a reference file, except `status`, which changes nothing in the plan or records.
- **The method, as structure.** Written backwards checks beside every answer and one closing *Least sure of: ___* line per sheet, in place of per-answer confidence flags. A 2-day recheck (44–72 hours) booked for everything taught. Mistakes on a 1-day, 3-day, 1-week and 3-week ladder, with wrong ideas fixed before they are re-tested cold. Mastery levels 0–5 computed from the record: same-day practice reaches at most level 2, and anything higher needs a cold recheck or a measurement.
- **`indelible.py`, a command-line tool** (Python 3.9+, standard library only) that is the only writer of the learner's data:
  - setup: `doctor`, `init`, `subject add`, `set`, `schema`;
  - session start: `brief` (at most 4,500 characters) and `due`;
  - the session lock, its time budget and the close checklist: `session open|status|expose|taught|override|close`;
  - sheets: specs, the sheet checker (`sheet lint`), rendering to PDF, HTML or Markdown, issuing, and evidence filing (`scan ingest`);
  - answer keys sealed apart, opened only by `key open` once an attempt is filed;
  - grading records (`grade record`), mistakes (`error ...`) and topics (`topic ...`);
  - plan blocks and their checks (`plan add|place|move|cancel|done|miss|list|week|check`);
  - calendar operations (`plan diff`, `cal ack`) and `.ics` export (`cal ics`);
  - the ledger of to-dos, decisions and Claude's own mistakes, `note append`, `stats`, `review week` and `compact`.
- **Sheet building by a builder subagent**, so answers never enter the main conversation.
- **A learner-owned workspace** of plain JSON, JSONL and Markdown files, with generated views and a `.gitignore` that keeps keys, typed answers, photos and the inbox out of version control.
- **Tests** (unittest) on macOS, Linux and Windows with Python 3.9 and 3.13, and CI.
- **Evals:** four synthetic learners (`evals/fixtures/persona-a…d.json`) and a trigger set (`evals/trigger.json`).
- **Maintainer tooling:** `dev/CONTRACT.md` (the build contract) and `dev/privacy_grep.py` (a pre-push privacy check: a local denylist, the handle allowlist, commit and tag identities, and optional text fingerprints).

- A claude.ai and mobile-app mode (a state file carried between chats).
- Two-way calendar sync. In 0.1, Claude proposes blocks, `plan check` validates them, and calendar changes go one way after a preview and a yes.
