An open-source skill for Claude Code

Learning that survives the cold test.

indelible runs your self-study the way a strict, kind tutor would. You read theory on a sheet and then close it. Claude marks your work from photos of your paper. Everything comes back cold two days later, and only what you still know then counts.

Your 2-day recheck is ready. Take it now

Install it in Claude Code

$ claude plugin marketplace add romanprigodskii/indelible
$ claude plugin install indelible@indelible

Then open Claude Code in an empty folder and say “set me up for…”, naming what you are learning. Needs Python 3.9 or later.

Not sure yet? Try a 2-minute sheet on this page and come back for the recheck.

Six rules, kept for you

These are the rules a good tutor holds you to. indelible keeps them in every session, including the days you would rather it didn’t.

  1. 1

    Read it, then close it.

    Theory comes on its own sheet. You read it and put it away before the drills, because an explanation left in view turns a test into a lookup.

  2. 2

    One kind at a time, then all mixed.

    Drills come in blocks of one question type. Anything that measures you is shuffled and unlabelled, like the real exam.

  3. 3

    Nothing counts until it survives 48 hours.

    Every new skill comes back cold two days later, on fresh problems. Same-day scores are practice.

  4. 4

    Check backwards, in writing.

    Beside every answer you write one check that runs the other way. Solving it again in your head repeats the same slip. A written check catches it.

  5. 5

    Every miss comes back.

    Each mistake goes in an error log and returns after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week and 3 weeks, until it stays.

  6. 6

    You do the work.

    You get the smallest hint that unblocks you, never an answer you could reach yourself. When an answer is wrong, you see where, and the fix is yours.

Read it, then close it

With the explanation in view, every answer is a lookup. It feels like knowing, and it isn’t.

So theory comes on its own sheet. You read it, close it, and only then get the drills. Reopening is allowed, but it is written down, and those answers count as practice.

      Claude, at your next session

      A preview in your browser. Nothing here is saved or sent anywhere, and no real calendar is touched.

      The first eight minutes

      A short interview, one question at a time, each with a default: your goal and date, where you are now, your materials, session length and days, when your head is clearest, when you sleep.

      You see a two-week plan first and the calendar question second. Nothing is written until you say yes to a one-screen summary. Then a diagnostic, before any teaching.

      Privacy policy · What it runs, writes and sends

      Honest limits

      A skill cannot physically stop the model from breaking its own rules, so the rules that matter most do not rely on instructions alone. The only route to a printable sheet runs a sheet checker. The only route to an answer key needs your attempt filed first. The next session finds anything the last one skipped, and does it first.

      The method was developed with one learner over about four weeks. That is not a controlled study. The principles come from well-studied effects: retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006), spacing (Cepeda et al., 2006) and interleaving (Rohrer & Taylor, 2007). The numbers, like 48 hours and six questions a block, are defaults you can change.
